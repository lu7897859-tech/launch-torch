## Article Body

---

Last month, my YouTube video got a 1,337% retention rate.

Sounds amazing, right? That's what I thought too — until I noticed it had 11 views.

That number sums up my last three months pretty well: a surreal mix of "this is technically working" and "almost nobody is watching."

I live in China. I don't have a Twitter following. I'm not a "creator." I'm a solo engineer who decided to build a Gumroad store with seven digital products — sourcing guides, Notion templates, an AI clustering tool — and try to sell them to a Western audience from the other side of the planet.

Here's the raw, unpolished version of how that's going.

---

## What I Built

Seven products sounds like a lot. It is. I probably should have stopped at three. But when you're building stuff alone at 2 AM, every idea feels like *the one*.

Here are two that represent the extremes of what I tried:

**The "serious" product**: *China Sourcing Playbook* ($19) — a 50+ page guide on how to find Chinese manufacturers, negotiate pricing, avoid common scams, and ship products internationally. This is the thing I actually have domain expertise in. I've been in and around manufacturing and hardware supply chains for years.

**The "engineer-trying-to-be-creative" product**: *Auto-AI-Cluster* ($29) — a tool that automatically groups and clusters text data using AI embeddings. Useful? Yes. Marketed well? Absolutely not.

The other five are in that same bucket: templates, checklists, technical micro-tools. I threw everything at the wall.

---

## The Weirdest Metric I've Ever Seen

That 1,337% retention rate on Episode 1 of my video series? It's not a typo. YouTube Studio counts it that way when people rewatch specific segments over and over. Out of 11 total views, someone (or a few someones) kept replaying a section.

Is that a good sign? Maybe. Is it a vanity metric that means nothing at 11 views? Also yes.

I screenshotted it. I'm not proud of that, but I did.

---

## Three Lessons I Didn't Want to Learn

### 1. Nobody cares about your product until they trust *you*

I launched seven products before I had seven followers. That's backwards. People on Gumroad don't buy from anonymous storefronts — they buy from people they've seen on Twitter, YouTube, or a newsletter for months.

Building trust takes longer than building the product. I knew this intellectually. I now know it in my bones.

### 2. SEO from China for a Western audience is brutal

I write in English. I'm targeting English-speaking buyers. But Google sees my site coming from a Chinese host, with no backlinks, no domain authority, and no social signals. I may as well be whispering into a black hole.

My traffic sources, in order: direct links I share manually → Reddit posts that get removed → organic search (negligible, like single digits per week). Growth hacking from 7,000 miles away with no existing network is just... hacking at air.

### 3. A free lead magnet only works if people can find it

I put together a genuinely useful free checklist — *"Importing From China: 15-Step Pre-Shipment Checklist"* — thinking it would be my funnel. Give away value, capture emails, sell the playbook.

Zero downloads organically. The only downloads came from me pasting the link into relevant Reddit threads before moderators took them down. The funnel works; the top of the funnel doesn't.

---

## Where I Am Now

Zero paid orders. Seven products. Eleven YouTube views. No audience.

And I'm still building.

Here's why: every week, I get slightly better at one thing. Last week I learned how to structure a Gumroad product page that doesn't look sketchy. This week I'm experimenting with short-form content. Next week I'll try something else.

The products themselves aren't bad — I genuinely believe the Sourcing Playbook is worth $19 to the right person. The problem is that "the right person" doesn't know I exist.

So the pivot isn't "quit and do something else." It's "stop treating this like a product launch and start treating it like a content business." Write first, build second. Trust first, sell second.

---

## The Honest Summary

If you're thinking about doing this — building digital products from outside the US/EU and selling to a Western audience — here's what I'd tell myself three months ago:

- Build **one** product. Spend the other 80% of your time on content and distribution.
- Your first 100 "customers" are actually just people who know your name. Go get those first.
- A 1,337% retention rate on 11 views is funny, but it doesn't pay rent.

I'm not giving up. I'm just finally doing it in the right order.
